## 1. Interview guides

### 1.0 Rules for every interview

Print this and keep it next to the guide.

**Show, don't tell.**
- Ask for the last real instance, not "usually". *"Tell me about the last time…"* beats *"How often do you…?"*
- Ask people to show: *"Could you open the app / message / notebook and walk me through it?"* Screens and phones are better evidence than memories.
- Ask for numbers only when they can check them (a calendar, an invoice, a message thread). Otherwise write "estimate".

**No leading questions.**
| Don't ask | Ask instead |
|---|---|
| "Wouldn't it help to get feedback between lessons?" | "What happened between your last two lessons?" |
| "Is practicing at home hard?" | "Where did you sing this week? Who could hear you?" |
| "Would you pay $29 for this?" | "What do you pay for today to solve this? What did you stop paying for, and why?" |
| "Do you hate admin?" | "Walk me through yesterday after your last lesson." |
| "Our idea is… what do you think?" | Don't pitch during the interview. If they ask, answer at the end (see "Last 3 minutes"). |

**Safety and respect.**
- 18+ only. If someone says they're under 18, thank them, stop, and delete any notes.
- Never judge a voice. If asked "Can I sing?" or "Am I too old?", say: *"I'm not a coach and this isn't an assessment — I'm here to learn from your experience. What I can say is that singing is a skill people learn at any age."*
- If someone describes pain, hoarseness lasting more than two weeks, or losing their voice: don't advise. Say: *"That sounds worth checking with a doctor or a voice specialist."* Note it ("mentioned", no details); don't probe further.
- It's fine to skip any question. Say so at the start.
- No selling in the interview. Founding Coach or lesson offers come only after the interview ends, only if they ask, or by a separate email with an easy "no thanks".

**Logistics.**
- 30 minutes. Guide times are targets; drop optional (◇) questions if short on time.
- Record only with consent (section 5). If no recording, take notes and ask permission to quote anonymously.
- Write the interview log (4.1) within 24 hours, while it's fresh.
- US/UK: mirror the person's words (practice/practise, student/pupil, semester/term, grades/exams).

**Last 3 minutes (every interview).**
1. *"Is there anything I should have asked but didn't?"*
2. *"Who else should I talk to?"* (ask them to introduce, not to give contact details)
3. *"Can I follow up by email if I have one more question?"*
4. Thank-you gift confirmation (not conditional on anything they said).

### 1.1 Voice teacher interview (30 min)

**Who:** private voice teachers, US first, then UK and AU. CCM / pop / musical theatre priority; 1–2 classical max. Teaches adults (at least some 18+ students). Online, in person, or hybrid.
**Goal:** what happens between lessons, how teachers see progress and early signs of leaving, unpaid time, tools they use and refuse. This is also how we meet potential Founding Coaches — but the interview is not a sales call.

**Intro (2 min)**
> "Thanks for making time. I'm [NAME] from Voice Family, a small voice studio that's exploring tools for the time between lessons. I'm not selling anything today — I want to understand how your teaching week actually works. There are no right answers, and you can skip anything. Is it OK if I record so I can listen back? The recording stays with our small team and is deleted after [90 days]."

**Warm-up (3 min)**
1. Tell me a bit about your studio: how many students, roughly what ages, which styles, online or in person?
2. How did most of your current students find you?

**Between lessons (8 min)** — *academic §5.2 q7, VT §4.4*
3. Think about last week. After a lesson ends, what does the student leave with? *(Show me: a recording, notes, a message, an email?)*
4. **Show me:** could you open your phone or email and walk me through how you answered students between lessons this week? How long did that take? Was any of it paid?
5. Of the students you taught last week, how do you know whether they practiced? What did you see or hear?
6. ◇ What do you do when a student comes back having practiced the wrong thing?
7. If you had to name the three biggest problems in your teaching week, what would they be? *(Write verbatim. Don't suggest options. Record the order.)* → **key for threshold: is "between lessons / practice" in their top 3?**

**Cues and the invisible instrument (5 min)** — *academic §5.2 q9*
8. **Show me** the three cues or gestures you repeat most often. *(Ask them to demo if comfortable.)*
9. How does a student remember that cue at home? What have you seen work or fail?
10. ◇ When a student says "I don't hear any difference", what do you do?

**Progress and leaving (6 min)** — *academic §5.2 q8, CHURN*
11. Think of the last adult student who stopped lessons. When did you first notice something was changing? What were the signs?
12. How did they tell you — or did they?
13. ◇ How do you currently show a student they've improved? Anything you record or keep?

**Voice health and your own voice (3 min)** — *academic §5.2 q12, TVOICE*
14. When did a student last mention their voice hurting or feeling hoarse? What did you do, and who did you send them to?
15. ◇ How is your own voice at the end of a teaching day? How many times a day do you demonstrate?

**Tools and money (3 min)** — *TOOLS, H6*
16. **Show me** the tools you use to run your studio (scheduling, billing, recordings). What do you pay for each month?
17. What's a tool you tried and stopped using? What made you stop?
18. ◇ Have you ever charged more for a lesson that includes something extra (a recording, a practice plan)? What happened? *(Don't name a price. Only ask about what they did.)*

**Last 3 minutes** — see 1.0.

**Only if they ask what we're building (after the interview):**
> "We're testing a simple loop with our own teachers: after a lesson the student gets a short practice task, sends one voice recording that week, and the coach replies by voice. We also keep a before/now pair so the student can hear the change. No scores, no student accounts, and the teacher approves every word. We're looking for a handful of Founding Coaches to try it with adult students. Would you like me to send details by email? No pressure at all."

**Note-taker fields:** country, styles, # adult students, online/hybrid, top-3 problems (verbatim, in order), minutes/week between lessons (shown or estimated), paid? (y/n), leaving signs (verbatim), tools + monthly cost, interest in Founding Coach (none / asked for info / wants to try).

### 1.2 Adult student interview (30 min) — current and lapsed

**Who:** adults 18+ who took voice lessons (private, online, group) in the last 24 months, or tried to learn with apps/YouTube. Quota: 3–5 who stopped lessons in the last 12 months ("lapsed"). Mix of beginners and returners; US first.
**Who does NOT interview:** the student's own teacher or the methodologist (plan 5.2). People speak more freely to a neutral person.
**Goal:** why adults stop, where they physically practice, reaction to hearing their own recording, trust in apps, what makes lessons feel worth the money.

**Intro (2 min)**
> "Thanks for joining. I'm [NAME], a researcher working with Voice Family, a small voice studio. I'm not a singing teacher and this isn't an assessment — nobody will hear you sing today, and there's nothing to get right. I'd like to hear about your experience with singing and lessons. You can skip any question. OK to record, so I can listen back? It stays with our small team and is deleted after [90 days]."

**Warm-up (3 min)**
1. How did singing come into your life? When did you decide to take lessons (or try to learn)?
2. What were you hoping singing would do for you?

**Where and how you practice (5 min)** — *academic §5.2 q2; H8*
3. Think about this past week (or the last week you were practicing). Where physically did you sing? Who could hear you?
4. What, if anything, did you do so you wouldn't be heard?
5. **Where can you practice?** *(read the four options exactly; tick one)* — A room where I can sing at normal volume / Only quietly (thin walls, family at home, neighbors) / Only in the car / Nowhere right now.

**Between lessons (5 min)** — *academic §5.2 q5, q6*
6. Walk me through the days between your last two lessons. What did you actually do?
7. Was there a moment you wanted to message your teacher between lessons and didn't? What stopped you?
8. ◇ What should happen between lessons so a lesson doesn't feel wasted?

**Hearing yourself (4 min)** — *academic §5.2 q3, REC*
9. Tell me about the first time you listened to a recording of your own singing. What did you feel? What did you do next?
10. What would help you hear more than just the mistakes?

**Apps and self-teaching (3 min, only if used)** — *academic §5.2 q4, APP/PAYW*
11. **Show me** a singing app you used, if it's still on your phone. When did you trust it? When did you stop?
12. ◇ Have you been charged for something you didn't expect? What happened?

**Stopping (lapsed only, 6 min — replaces q6–8 if short on time)** — *academic §5.2 q1 (main gap)*
13. Think back to the week you decided not to book another lesson. What was going on that week — and in the month before?
14. Was there a single moment you remember? What did you tell your teacher, if anything?
15. Looking back, what — if anything — would have kept you going? *(Don't offer options.)*
16. ◇ Did you keep singing after you stopped lessons? How?
→ **Key for threshold:** does the person, unprompted, describe not noticing progress / not knowing if they were improving? Code verbatim; do not ask "Was it because you didn't see progress?"

**Money (2 min)** — *academic §5.2 q5*
17. What did you pay per lesson, and how often did you go? How did that feel?
18. What frequency of lessons is realistic for you money-wise?

**Voice health (1 min, optional)** — *academic §5.2 q12*
19. ◇ Has your voice ever hurt or felt hoarse after singing? What did you do, and who, if anyone, did you tell?

**Last 3 minutes** — see 1.0. Do not pitch lessons. If they ask, send the landing page link by email afterwards, once.

**Note-taker fields:** age band (18–29 / 30–44 / 45–59 / 60+), country, beginner/returner, lessons status (current / lapsed / never), practice place (4 options), stopping story (verbatim), "didn't notice progress" mentioned unprompted (y/n), first-recording reaction (verbatim), apps used + trust/stop moment, price paid & frequency.

### 1.3 Studio / school owner interview (30 min)

**Who:** owners or managers of private music schools/studios that offer voice lessons, with ≥2 teachers. US first. *Per plan: month 3, only if owner hours allow (decision #3).*
**Goal:** what the school pays for today; what happens when a teacher leaves; whether "student history handoff" or "visible growth" ranks in their top 3. Feeds gate #4 (≥3 of 8 owners rank handoff in top 3 and ≥2 agree to a pilot).

**Intro (2 min)**
> "Thanks for your time. I'm [NAME] from Voice Family, a small voice studio. We're researching how music schools run the voice side of their business. I'm not here to sell — I'd like to understand how things work for you today. OK to record? It stays with our team and is deleted after [90 days]."

**Warm-up (3 min)**
1. Tell me about your school: how many teachers, how many voice students, what ages, in person or online?
2. What does a normal Monday look like for you?

**Tools and spend (6 min)**
3. **Show me** the software you use to run the school. What do you pay for each, roughly, per month?
4. Which of those would you fight to keep? Which would you drop tomorrow?
5. Tell me about the last time you switched or dropped a tool. Why?

**Teachers leaving (8 min)** — *academic §5.2 q11*
6. Think of the last teacher who left. What did you lose? What happened to their students over the next two months?
7. How was each student's history passed to the new teacher? **Show me** what that looked like, if there's anything.
8. ◇ How did you find out how many of those students stayed?

**Quality and progress (6 min)**
9. How do you know whether a voice teacher's students are progressing? What do you look at?
10. How do parents or adult students tell you they're happy — or not?
11. If you had to name your three biggest problems running the school this year, what would they be? *(verbatim, in order — don't suggest)* → **key for gate #4.**

**Paying for it (2 min)**
12. What's the last thing you paid for to fix one of those problems? How did you decide it was worth it?

**Last 3 minutes** — see 1.0. If they ask what we're building: describe the same loop as in 1.1 in one sentence; say it's a small pilot with adult students only; offer a follow-up email, no call booking pushed.

**Note-taker fields:** # teachers, # voice students, % adults (estimate), tools + monthly cost, teacher-left story (verbatim), handoff method, top-3 (verbatim, in order), handoff in top 3 (y/n), open to a small pilot later (no / maybe / yes).

## 3. Recruitment and community outreach (VF-015)

### 3.0 Outreach rules

1. **Communities and personal connections only.** No cold mass email, no bought lists, no scraping member directories. (One team emailed 500 active singing teachers and 3 replied — VT TOOLS; cold outreach also burns trust.)
2. **Ask the admin/moderator first.** Every post in a group, forum, newsletter or chapter goes out only with written permission. If a community has a "promotions day" or research thread, use only that.
3. **Always disclose.** Every message says who we are: *"I'm [NAME] from Voice Family, a small voice studio."* If you're a member of the group yourself, say you're posting on behalf of Voice Family.
4. **One post per community per wave.** No bumping. Reply to comments; don't start DM threads with people who didn't reach out.
5. **One follow-up maximum** to a person who didn't reply, after 7 days. Then stop.
6. **Reddit: we don't promote and don't recruit.** We may read public threads for research. We do not post, comment or DM about Voice Family, surveys or interviews on Reddit.
7. **No pressure, no scarcity.** No "limited spots", countdowns or "last chance". If a pilot is full, say so plainly and offer a waitlist.
8. **No claims we can't back.** No statistics, testimonials or "teachers love it". We haven't earned them yet.
9. **18+ only** for all interviews and surveys.
10. **Keep a contact log:** community, admin permission (date, who), post date, # replies, # interviews booked. This is the VF-015 metric (conversations → pilot).

### 4.4 How to update the master plan after a wave

Follow `reports/voice-family-product-plan.md` §10. Step by step:

1. **Freeze the wave.** Close the log; finish double-coding and quote checks before any conclusions.
2. **Write the wave summary (4.3).** Numbers as "k of n"; data with n<30 is at most reliability B and carries n.
3. **Compare with thresholds fixed in advance** (plan §6, "Исследования"; gate #4). Don't change a threshold after seeing the data — if a threshold was wrong, note it and change it for the *next* wave in the version log.
4. **Move assumptions.** For each [Д] touched, write the new label and the source ("interviews T-01…T-12, wave 1"). Candidates: T1 for voice; H2 priority; H8 (quiet share); £24 / "pupil" / "term" for UK; FC price $29; owners' handoff (H11).
5. **Contradictions go to plan §3 as a new row (№37+)**, never as a silent edit.
6. **Backlog:** update `reports/voice-family-backlog.csv` — new ideas get the next VF-ID; nothing is reused; items dropped move to «Не делаем» with the reason. Update evidence/score columns for VF-015, VF-087 and anything whose evidence changed.
7. **Capacity check:** anything added to a stage → recount developer days and methodologist hours and name what's dropped.
8. **Constraints don't move for data:** voice safety, privacy, 18+, AI Gate, ≤20 min teacher time.
9. **Version log** at the end of the plan: date, what changed, which wave.
10. **Owner decisions:** if a wave touches plan §9 items (e.g., #11 UK second, #12 schools go/no-go), put the recommendation and the k-of-n evidence in front of the owner.

Schedule: wave 1 summary — end of week 2 (first ≥5 teacher conversations; plan exit gate "≥5 conversations booked"); wave 2 — week 8; wave 3 (survey + owners) — month 3.
